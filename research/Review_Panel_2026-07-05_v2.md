## CRITICAL ISSUES (Devil's Advocate; block acceptance)

### C1 — The cost axis is API market price, not resource cost, yet it is billed as the most robust axis. [Devil's Advocate CRITICAL + Methodology MAJOR + Perspective MAJOR]
The abstract/§5.1 lead with "measured cost ... needs no energy assumption at all," but all models (open included) are billed through one API gateway (§3.3), so USD/task is provider list price. For open models on competitive multi-host gateways that price may be at or below serving cost (a business artifact), not physical efficiency. The tell is internal: DeepSeek-vs-Claude price ratio ~26x but the paper's own energy ratio ~4.7x; Gemma-vs-Claude ~110x price vs ~39x energy. Price and modeled resource use diverge 3–5x. **Fix:** relabel the axis "current API list price," soften "most robust" and "no frontier model is Pareto-optimal" to pricing-contingent, foreground energy or tokens/task as the provider-neutral axis, and ideally model on-prem TCO (amortized H200 + electricity at the measured concurrency-1 utilization) for the two locally-served models and show the ordering survives.

### C2 — The reasoning comparison is asymmetric. [Devil's Advocate CRITICAL + Methodology MAJOR]
§4.5 runs all three frontier models with reasoning to reach parity (Gemini 0.677 ≈ DeepSeek-direct 0.676), but the open models are never run with reasoning, even though DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5 have native reasoning modes. If DeepSeek-reasoning gains the same +0.03–0.05, it lands ~0.71–0.72 and the "parity" narrative changes (plausibly in the paper's favor). "The frontier does not surpass the best open model even when reasoning" is only true because the open model is handicapped. **Fix:** run DeepSeek-V3.2 (and ideally GLM-5) with reasoning enabled for a symmetric matched-reasoning comparison, or restate the conclusion explicitly as "frontier-with-reasoning vs open-direct" and flag the asymmetry as a threat to the parity claim.

### C3 — Publication-blocking placeholders. [EIC CRITICAL]
Data Availability is "[repository URL]" (line 210); author is "Firstname Lastname" (26); affiliation is a placeholder (31). The reproducibility claim is load-bearing. **Fix:** archived repository with a DOI (e.g. Zenodo) plus complete author/affiliation metadata and model version/pricing snapshot dates. (Author's to supply.)

## MAJOR ISSUES

- **M1 — Holm family is data-selected (9 = top-3 open × 3, not 15 = all 5 × 3).** [Stats MAJOR + DA MAJOR + Methodology MINOR] Under a pre-specified 15-comparison family, Gemma-vs-Claude (p=0.03) flips to ~0.06 (non-significant); DeepSeek's p<0.001 survives. **Fix:** state the 9-family was fixed a priori (defensible: the excluded pairs are all null and only inflate m), or report under the full 15, and note the Gemma-vs-Claude sensitivity.
- **M2 — "DeepSeek p<0.001" not independently confirmable; report exact per-comparison Holm p.** [Stats MAJOR-verify] DeepSeek-vs-Gemini is the smallest gap; the direction is robust at α=0.05 under any family, but the "<0.001" label should be the exact adjusted p or "≤ 2/20000".
- **M3 — Frontier energy uncertainty compounds and the carbon figures ignore it.** [Methodology MAJOR + Perspective MAJOR + DA MAJOR] Frontier energy scales in both ε (one GPT-4o anchor) and N_active (~100B assumption); the sensitivity band varies only N_active. The carbon paragraph (§5.1) uses Claude's point-value 2489 J while §4.3/limitations mandate ranges, an internal inconsistency. Gemma's 64 J is also estimated, so the ratio is estimate-vs-estimate. **Fix:** present frontier energy and carbon as ranges everywhere, state the two uncertainties compound multiplicatively, and note the headline ratio is estimate-vs-estimate.
- **M4 — "Small open models dominate" conflates the quality winner (DeepSeek, a ~671B-total MoE) with the efficiency winner (Gemma-4B).** [DA MAJOR] DeepSeek is not "small" and exceeds single-GPU capacity. **Fix:** separate the claims: Gemma is the efficiency endpoint; DeepSeek is the quality winner.
- **M5 — Devign used three times (lines 101, 134, 196) but never cited.** [Domain MAJOR] **Fix:** add Zhou et al., Devign, NeurIPS 2019 (verify before adding).
- **M6 — PrimeVul's paired/VD-Score protocol is deferred, but it is the control for the paper's own top contamination threat.** [Domain MAJOR] **Fix:** run pairwise correctness or VD-Score for the top 3–4 models on the paired subset, or justify the rebalanced-sample choice far more strongly.
- **M7 — Single prompt template; prompt sensitivity untested.** [Domain MAJOR] The paper cites SecLLMHolmes (whose finding is prompt non-robustness) and the ranking is flagging-propensity-driven. **Fix:** run 2–3 prompt variants for the top models and report ranking stability, or add an explicit robustness limitation.
- **M8 — Truncation budget unquantified.** [Domain MAJOR + Methodology MINOR] The character budget, the fraction truncated, and whether the labeled vulnerable line survives are never stated; if it is cut, the input no longer supports the label. **Fix:** report all three plus a sensitivity check on untruncated functions.
- **M9 — On MCC (threshold-invariant), the open lead largely collapses; only DeepSeek clearly leads.** [Domain MAJOR + DA] Gemini 0.298 ≈ Gemma 0.296 > GPT 0.230. **Fix:** foreground MCC in the ranking discussion, temper the multi-model "open leads," and consider eliciting confidence/logprobs for a scored metric (ROC-AUC / VD-Score).
- **M10 — Single draw / single seed / single generation; API run-to-run variance unquantified.** [Methodology MAJOR + DA MAJOR + EIC MINOR] The bootstrap covers within-sample sampling error only, not which 1000 safe functions were drawn nor API nondeterminism. **Fix:** ≥3–5 repeated generations for 2–3 models and/or one alternative safe-pool draw; report the variance.
- **M11 — Latency is in the title and called first-class but yields no result.** [Structure MAJOR + EIC MINOR] It is demoted three times to a confounded "directional observation." **Fix (lower-risk):** drop "Latency" from the title to "Cost- and Energy-Aware" and reframe it as a secondary observation in the abstract, or run a controlled single-request latency pass.
- **M12 — The non-LLM baselines "all methods are weak" leg is never foreshadowed in the Introduction.** [Structure MAJOR] It surfaces abruptly in §3.4/5.2. **Fix:** add one intro sentence (or a fourth contribution) previewing the non-LLM reference points and the "far from deployment-ready" finding.
- **M13 — Flawfinder is the precision winner at realistic prevalence, which complicates "best LLMs beat both baselines."** [DA MAJOR] "Best LLMs beat both" holds only on balanced accuracy; Flawfinder (6%) beats every LLM (2.3–3.8%) on precision@1:44. **Fix:** state this plainly at the comparison, and mark the learned baseline as a cross-dataset non-transfer artifact rather than a learned-detector ceiling.
- **M14 — Single dataset / language / task caps external validity.** [EIC MAJOR] **Fix:** add one corroborating dataset (SecVulEval, already cited) on a reduced model set to show the efficiency ordering generalizes, or scope the title and claims to PrimeVul / C-C++.
- **M15 — Novelty margin over Neef/Dahiya is thin and rests on estimated energy.** [EIC MAJOR + Domain MINOR] **Fix:** add a compact comparison table of prior efficiency-aware security benchmarks (axes × models × datasets) to make the delta concrete.
